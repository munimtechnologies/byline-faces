# byline-faces survey 5: one site, real staff faces replaced by invented ones (pre-registration)

Written 2026-11-24, before any data for this survey was collected and before any result of
surveys 1-4 was seen (their jobs have not started). Licence: CC BY 4.0 (text). Made with AI
agents working for MV2 of Munim, Inc.; no human expert has reviewed this design yet.

## Why this survey

Surveys 1-4 compare writer headshots across different sites, years and image pipelines.
That leaves a confound: a detector can separate two sets of pictures because of their
compression, size or cropping, not because one set is AI-made. A clean test needs real and
invented writer faces shown **by the same site, on the same page template, through the same
image pipeline**. One documented case provides exactly that.

## The case (all from published reporting)

- **Kotaku** (Lewis Parker, with Ethan Gach, 26 Feb 2026,
  https://kotaku.com/resident-evil-requiem-metacritic-ai-review-videogamer-2000674042):
  VideoGamer, recently sold to Clickout Media, published a Resident Evil Requiem review under
  "Brian Merrygold", who "appeared to be a made-up person complete with an AI profile image";
  the image file was titled "ChatGPT-Image-Oct-20-2025-11_57_34-AM-300×300.png". Other
  current bylines (Shooter Orson, Steven Danielson) had "similarly suspicious-looking
  AI-generated profile pictures", their X accounts were all created in October 2025, and
  bylines from the site's 15-year history were "overwritten with fake AI profiles".
- **Press Gazette** (Rob Waugh, 27 Feb 2026,
  https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/staff-journalists-sacked-and-misleadingly-replaced-with-ai-writers/):
  Clickout sites "misleadingly replaced" laid-off staff "with AI writers, complete with fake
  pics and biogs"; Merrygold's picture and "Callum Mercer"'s were rated AI-generated by
  IdentifAI.
- **Press Gazette** (Nov 2026, summarised by ThePrint on 20 Nov 2026,
  https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/bots-are-replacing-reporters-in-cryptocurrency-journalism/):
  ICO Bench, one of a group of crypto sites believed to be owned or controlled by Clickout
  Media/Finixio, carries "Patrick Johnson", whose profile image, biography and much of whose
  output appear to be AI-generated.

The Merrygold file name is, as far as we know, the only fake-journalist headshot in a
published case whose generator is named by the site's own uploaded file (OpenAI's ChatGPT
image generation, Oct 2025). In surveys 2-4 the origin labels come from detectors, experts'
judgement, a hoaxer's own statement (DALL-E, Irish Times 2023) or a marketplace listing
(Generated Photos, Sports Illustrated 2023); none of those is a post-2024 generator.

## Design

Data: Wayback Machine captures of `videogamer.com/author/*` pages (CDX prefix query; the
index folds "www."), and the headshot image each page shows, fetched from the archive.

- **Before** = captures dated before 2025-09-01 (before the first reported AI content on the
  site, "fall 2025", and before the October 2025 accounts).
- **After** = captures dated 2026-02-19 or later (the week of the reported layoffs onward).
- Captures between the two dates are recorded but not used in V1-V4.
- Unit = one distinct headshot (SHA-256 of the image bytes) per period; the same picture
  shown on several pages counts once. Pages without a single detected face are skipped.
- Tests, unchanged from surveys 2-4: Community Forensics (Park and Owens, CVPR 2025;
  weights OwensLab/commfor-model-384), flag threshold 0.5, after the same reproduction check
  (if the reproduction check fails, every CF prediction is "invalid"); and the StyleGAN
  eye-placement test (Yang et al. 2024) with survey 1's calibrated tolerance.
- Secondary persona pages (not in V1-V4): `videogamer.com/author/brian-merrygold/`,
  `.../shooter-orson/`, `.../steven-danielson/` (all archived 2026-03-01),
  `esports-news.co.uk/author/callummercer/` (archived 2026-04-07),
  `icobench.com/author/patrickjohnson/` (current avatar uploaded May 2025, shown at 50x50 px;
  the job tries the same upload without the size suffix and records the pixel size it
  scored).

## Predictions (fixed now)

- **V1 (separation):** share of After headshots CF-flagged >= 0.50, and share of Before
  headshots CF-flagged <= 0.15.
- **V2 (ranking):** the CF scores of After headshots are higher than those of Before
  headshots with a Mann-Whitney AUC >= 0.80.
- **V3 (named generator):** the Merrygold headshot (any capture whose image path contains
  "ChatGPT-Image") has a CF score >= 0.50.
- **V4 (not StyleGAN):** at most one After headshot is flagged by the StyleGAN eye test
  (we expect the newer generator's faces not to share StyleGAN's fixed eye placement).
- **Minimum sample:** V1, V2 and V4 are graded only if each period has at least 5 distinct
  headshots; otherwise "unresolved: too few archived pictures", reported with the counts.
- A failed prediction is reported as failed. If V1 or V2 fails while V3 holds, the
  report says the detector saw the named-generator face but did not separate the periods,
  and lists the After pictures it missed (hash and score only).

## What would be new

No published study we know of tests an AI-face detector on a site's own before-and-after
writer headshots, with one face whose generator is named by its file. Closest work:
Yang et al. 2024 (GAN faces among X profile pictures, one moment), Ricker et al. 2024
(AI-generated profile pictures on X), DFRLab 2025 (influence operations moving from GAN to
diffusion faces on social accounts), and Park and Owens 2025 (the detector itself, tested on
benchmark images). This survey runs only after surveys 1-4 have results, so their fixes
(page prefixes, reproduction check) carry over; its code will ship with the v0.6 release.

## Privacy

Real former staff are not named in any output. Before-period pictures appear only as
counts and scores; their images are never published. Invented personas named in the
reporting above may be named.
